## Global design system

Use site fonts and branding as-is. Don't introduce new typefaces.

**Minimum text sizes — nothing smaller than these**

| Element | 1:1 (1080px) | 4:5 (1080×1350) | 9:16 (1080×1920) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Persona tag | 28px | 30px | 34px |
| Headline | 64px | 70px | 80px |
| Sub-headline (9:16 only) | — | — | 36px |
| Product name label | 22px | 24px | 26px |
| Price badge | 24px | 26px | 28px |
| Offer pill text | 22px | 24px | 26px |
| CTA button text | 26px | 28px | 30px |
| Brand name | 22px | 24px | 26px |

These are minimums. Go larger if space allows — these ads are viewed on phone screens at scroll speed. If any text feels small when the ad is viewed at phone size, increase it.

**Layout structure (top to bottom)**
1. Top bar — persona tag (left) + "LayerWeaver" brand (right)
2. Headline — 1–2 lines, punchy statement; accent colour on key phrase
3. Sub-headline — 9:16 only, muted colour, one line of supporting context
4. Product grid — 2×2 cells
5. Bottom bar — offer pills (left) + "Shop now →" CTA (right)

**Product cells**
- Rounded corners: 12px
- Centered image with padding inside
- Product name label: bottom-left, semi-transparent white background
- Price badge: bottom-right, accent colour background, white text

**Offer pills**
- Pill-shaped chips, left-aligned
- One pill highlighted in accent colour
- Rest in neutral/white with a light border

**CTA:** "Shop now →" — solid accent colour, white text, pill shape

**Spacing**
- Outer padding: 44–48px all sides
- Cell gap: 6px
- Pill gap: 12–16px

**Layout adaptation by ratio**
- 1:1: Product grid ~55% of height, headline ~52px
- 4:5: Taller cells, headline 56–60px, more bottom padding
- 9:16: Headline 64–72px, sub-headline visible, cells much taller, pills can wrap to 2 rows

## Ad 1 — The Gamer Bro

### Why this ad works
Brothers are the hardest person to shop Rakhi gifts for. Generic gifting ads don't speak to them at all. A gaming-specific persona makes the buyer feel immediately understood — "yes, that's exactly my brother." The products are also genuinely useful desk objects he'd actually want, not novelty Rakhi items.

### Visual direction
Arrange all 4 products on a dark surface — matte black or dark grey. Subtle overhead lighting. Tight, organised layout. This isn't a cheerful gifting aesthetic — it's a "this is a cool desk" aesthetic. The dark background of the ad should feel like it continues from the photo background.

### Hook options — A/B test these

| Hook | Opening visual | Opening line |

| A — Contrast | Chocolate box crossed out, then product grid appears | "Not this. This." |
| B — Direct | Cactus Headphone Stand extreme close-up, pull back | "He's been wanting something for his setup." |
| C — Call-out | Dark screen, then text types: "For the brother who games." | "For the brother who games." |

## Ad 2 — The Aesthetic Sister

### Why this ad works
"Aesthetic" is a clearly understood identity category for young women — they self-identify this way. Targeting an identity rather than a product type makes the ad feel personally addressed. The Ghost Balloon Lamp is also a proven visual converter in your catalog. Warm, editorial visual tone fits the persona.

### Visual direction
Arrange products on a cream or linen surface. Use natural light from one side (window light, not harsh overhead). Products should be loosely arranged, not rigid. If you have a small plant or book nearby, put it in. The frame should feel like something from a Pinterest board, not a product shoot.

## Ad 3 — Little Things, Big Feels

### Why this ad works
Not every buyer has a large budget. The ₹299–₹499 price range has the least friction to convert — impulse buy territory. The collection name itself ("Little Things, Big Feels") is already the emotional hook. This ad captures everyone who wants to give something but is budget-conscious or buying for multiple people.

### Visual direction
Tight flat lay from directly above. White or light cream surface. All 4 products arranged in a small cluster — close together, almost touching. Shoot in bright even light (midday near a window). The products are small, so the composition should feel full, not sparse.

## Ad 4 — The Kid Sibling

### Why this ad works
Buying for a younger sibling — especially a child or teen — is a very specific gifting problem. Most Rakhi gift ads are adult-to-adult. A kid-specific persona is completely unaddressed in the market. The products are also genuinely novel (3D printed articulated figures, a musical ocarina, a physics-based game) which means the visual creative can be genuinely surprising.

### Visual direction
Arrange products on a bright surface — light blue, yellow, or mint. Products spread out with personality, not a rigid grid. Position the T-Rex mid-stride and the Octopus with legs spread — show that these move.

### Hook options — A/B test these
